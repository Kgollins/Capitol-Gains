The first atlas: what the explored years show

Built in November 2026 from 2 January 2015 to 29 December 2023, 900 stocks, code version bb48488 (the build's report: record-build-20261112T230732Z-bb48488.json). These are the explored years only. The years 2024 to 2026 stay closed. Nothing here trades, and nothing here is a forecast.

The numbers

  • Attention. On a typical day 95.4% of stocks were quiet, 1.1% loud and 3.5% tired. A loud spell lasted 3 trading days (the middle value).
  • Catalysts. 6.6% of stock-days had a catalyst: an earnings report, a burst of news or a large price move. The mix changes over the years. Earnings reports are almost absent before 2020 (7 to 13 a year, then about 1,150 in 2020), and news bursts start in 2021, so catalysts roughly double from 2021 (about 9,400 to 11,200 a year before 2020, 15,200 in 2020, then 18,400 to 20,900 a year). Before 2020 nearly all catalysts are large price moves.
  • Same-day pull. On the day of a catalyst, stocks linked by shared news moved about +0.091 usual daily swings with it (between +0.083 and +0.098), and stocks linked by moving together moved about +0.208 (between +0.198 and +0.218), beyond similar stocks with no link. The real map beat 20 of 20 maps with the links shuffled at random inside sectors. Against maps shuffled across any sector, the looser test that sector news alone can beat, it also beat 20 of 20.
  • Follow or reverse.
  • One trading day later, stocks linked by moving together had moved about +0.0057 more in the catalyst's direction (between +0.0017 and +0.0097). This is a small carry-on, clear of zero. Shared news read about −0.0002 (between −0.0053 and +0.0050).
  • Five trading days later, linked stocks had moved about −0.001 (between −0.005 and +0.003) for shared news and about +0.002 (between −0.002 and +0.005) for moving together. After 20 days the figures were about −0.002 (between −0.008 and +0.003) and about −0.001 (between −0.004 and +0.003).
  • These four are zero within their error bars: beyond the first day, the same-day move neither carries on nor turns back.
  • Aftershocks.
  • Moves together, the cleaner figure: the day after a catalyst, a linked stock was about +0.58 points more likely than a look-alike to have a catalyst of its own (between +0.51 and +0.64). Ten days later the gap was about +0.02 (between −0.05 and +0.08).
  • Shared news: the first-day gap was about +2.3 points (between +2.1 and +2.6), and it was still about +1.6 after ten days.
  • Read the shared-news figure as an upper bound. A shared-news link is made from stories that name both stocks, and the same stories feed both stocks' news bursts, so part of it is built in.
  • Attention spreading.
  • Within 5 days after a stock turned loud, about +0.28 points more of its moving-together linked stocks turned loud than look-alikes (between +0.14 and +0.41). This is the cleaner figure.
  • For shared-news links the figure was about +2.4 points (between +2.1 and +2.7). It is an upper bound for the same reason as above: the stories that make the link also feed both stocks' attention.
  • A clean test of attention spreading along shared-news links comes with the lenses, stage 4.
  • Cascades.
  • A large catalyst is 4.4 usual swings or more. 51.8% of large catalysts followed a large catalyst at a linked stock in the 5 days before, against 49.0% on shuffled maps that keep links inside sectors (48.4% to 49.7% across the 20 maps). On maps shuffled across any sector the figure was 46.8% to 48.2%, the looser test.
  • Chains of 10 or more large catalysts were 2.5% of chains on the real map, against 2.3% on maps shuffled inside sectors and 2.0% on maps shuffled across sectors. The map's shape alone gives more long chains than the shuffled maps do (check 1), so this gap is not evidence of spreading.
  • In step. At the end of March 2020 the in-step dial read +0.63, against a middle value of +0.32 in 2019.
  • Roughness. The market's Hurst number had a middle value of +0.48. One half means no memory, and readings between 0.40 and 0.60 show no clear lean. A change in how wild the moves are pulls the number down on its own.
  • Flocking.
  • Year by year, from 2016 to 2023, how strongly the three flocking rules held among linked little-known stocks:
  • heading the way linked stocks head: +0.011, −0.008, −0.006, +0.017, −0.010, +0.046, +0.004, +0.003.
  • drifting back toward them: −0.023, −0.016, +0.008, −0.033, +0.019, −0.082, −0.009, +0.007.
  • pushing apart when bunched: −0.012, +0.004, −0.011, +0.012, +0.009, −0.004, −0.006, +0.009.
  • Two of these 24 readings are clear of zero, both in 2021: heading the same way above zero and drifting back below it. That is about what chance alone gives (about one in twenty). 2021 is also the year shared-news links join the map.
  • On maps shuffled inside sectors the yearly averages were −0.004 to +0.004, −0.016 to +0.007 and −0.021 to +0.010.
  • Over all the weeks, heading the way linked stocks head read +0.0071 on the real map, above all 20 shuffled maps (−0.0062 to +0.0063). Drifting back read −0.016, above only 4 of 20; pushing apart read +0.0001, above 18 of 20.
  • The flock's alignment over the eight weeks to 29 December 2023: +0.015, +0.015, +0.009, +0.009, +0.013, +0.017, +0.013, +0.001, against about −0.002 on the shuffled maps. Over all the weeks it read +0.011, against −0.0025 to −0.0022 on maps shuffled inside sectors.

Checks on the instrument

The scripts and outputs behind these checks are kept beside this note, in checks-2026-11-12.

  • The two leans from the shape of the map. These tests place made-up events on the real links, so nothing can spread. Any gap between the real map and the shuffled maps is a lean of the instrument.
  • *Hot spots.* Made-up "hot" stocks (30, 45, 60 or 90 a day, 300 draws each) were placed on the links of June 2016, June 2019 and June 2022. When the hot stocks were drawn at random, the real map read between 0.04 lower and about the same as maps shuffled inside sectors (for example 0.81 against 0.81 to 0.82 with 90 hot stocks in 2022). When most of them came from one sector, the real map read 0.01 to 0.10 lower than maps shuffled inside sectors, but 0.05 to 0.14 higher than maps shuffled across sectors. So the hot-spots dial leans low against its main comparison.
  • *Large cascades.* 30 sets of made-up independent large catalysts were placed on the real links at each year's real rate (4.6 to 5.8 a day), against 20 maps shuffled inside sectors. The real map's share of cascades of 10 or more stood above the shuffled maps' average by +0.0002 (standard error 0.0002) in 2016, +0.0004 (0.0002) in 2019 and +0.0008 (0.0004) in 2022. That is 15% to 63% of the shuffled maps' share, and above 16, 20 and 18 of the 20 maps. The chains of made-up events are much smaller than real chains, but this upward lean is at least as large, relative to its size, as the real map's excess on the cascade-size chart (about 10%). So the cascade-size excess cannot be read as spreading. At a higher rate (12.7 a day), an earlier review measured the lean the other way.
  • The share of catalysts with a parent showed no lean (real against shuffled: +0.242 against +0.237 to +0.244, +0.229 against +0.223 to +0.231, +0.330 against +0.329 to +0.339).

The share with a parent reads +0.340 here, as a mean of weekly shares, and 51.8% in "The numbers", counted over all large catalysts together. Quiet weeks, with few large catalysts, have fewer parents.

  • Weeks with no flocking reading. Counted from 4 January 2016, none of the 415 weeks lacked a reading of the three rules, on the real map or on any of the 40 shuffled maps. With each daily move capped at 5 usual swings (10,322 moves capped), the flock's alignment over all the weeks read +0.0103, against +0.0107 uncapped (470 weeks, 2,264 days). So a few wild moves do not make the reading.
  • Look-alikes two links away. The record keeps up to 20 stocks two links from a catalyst's stock as echoes; stocks two links away beyond those 20 can still serve as look-alikes. On every fifth trading day (453 days, 23,487 catalysts, 370,224 linked stocks), such stocks filled 42% of look-alike places, and 0.8% of them have a link of their own to the catalyst's stock. On the catalyst's day they moved +0.013 usual swings more in its direction than the other look-alikes (−0.010 against −0.023). This shrinks "beyond look-alikes" by about 0.005 swings, which is small beside same-day pulls of +0.09 and +0.21. The shuffled maps share the same bias.
  • The links. In the middle month, 1.0% of all possible pairs were linked in 2015 to 2020 (about 8,100 links), and 1.8% from 2021, when shared-news links join (about 15,000 links). From 2021, 5.0% of linked pairs were on both kinds of link. 29% of links joined two stocks of the same sector (28% before 2021, 31% from 2021). 44% of links were returned, that is, A linked to B and B to A (43% before 2021, 47% from 2021). These figures cover 108 months with links.
  • Attention.
  • Of 5,026 entries into tired, 84 (1.7%) came straight from a quiet day; the rest followed a loud day. A tired day need not follow a loud trading day: attention is read on calendar days, so a loud spell can fall on a weekend or holiday between two trading days.
  • Of 5,400 loud spells, 894 (16.6%) lasted one trading day.
  • News bursts (29,330, from 2021) fell 18.4% on Mondays, 21.5% on Tuesdays, 21.4% on Wednesdays, 22.1% on Thursdays and 16.6% on Fridays, against 18.6%, 20.5%, 20.5%, 20.3% and 20.1% of trading days.
  • The in-step dial's middle value was +0.32 for the whole market and +0.33 to +0.63 for single sectors (middle +0.43; 11 sectors, 24,904 sector readings). A smaller group reads higher, so a sector's dial is read only against its own history.
  • Hot spots. Read on each week's last trading day, the share of hot stocks in one joined patch averaged +0.387 on the real map (middle value +0.309; 432 weeks; a middle week had 17 hot stocks). Maps shuffled inside sectors averaged +0.294 to +0.309, and maps shuffled across sectors +0.273 to +0.290. The lean of check 1 pushes the real map lower than the inside-sector maps, so the real map's excess stands against that lean, not because of it.
  • The first days of the record.
  • Links exist from the first month: January 2015's moves-together links rest on the prices of 2014. Cascade members start on 2 January 2015, and the contagion dial gives its first reading on 15 January 2015, once 30 large catalysts are behind it. The atlas shows it from 10 April 2015, once its 60 days, and the 5 days before them where parents are sought, lie inside the record.
  • In the first 5 trading days of January 2015, none of the 18 large catalysts had a parent on the real map, and 0 to 5 of 18 did on the shuffled maps. In the rest of that month 51 of 109 had one (29% to 50% on the shuffled maps). Those first days read low on every map because their 5-day window reaches back into December 2014, which has no links.

What this does not show yet

The fitted readouts (the R number, the branching ratio, the predator-prey rates) come with the lenses, stage 4. So does a clean test of attention and aftershocks along shared-news links. The live section comes with the recorder.

Cautions

The stock list is today's index members, with today's sectors, applied to the past; this flatters every result in the same way. Shared-news links start in 2021. Attention rests on Wikipedia views from mid-2015 and news counts from 2021. Catalysts roughly double from 2021, when earnings reports and news bursts are both counted.

Loud means attention rising sharply. It is rare: about 1% of stock-days, 0.7% before 2021 and 1.6% from 2021, when news counts join the reading. That step at 2021 is in the inputs, not the market, and every attention chart marks it. The build's check on the loud share first had a floor of 2%, and the first run stopped there, at 1.1%. After seeing that first run, the owner lowered the floor to 0.5%, because the 2% floor was a guess made for a wider definition of loud. Because loud is rare, attention-spreading figures rest on few cases, especially before 2021.
