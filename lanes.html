Lanes

The account trades ten lanes. A lane is a fixed rule that picks stocks after every close and trades its own slice of a paper account at the broker. Each rule was frozen before any result counted, and a change starts a new version from zero.

What every lane shares

The ten lanes use the same money rules and the same limits. Only what they look for, and how long they hold, is different.

MoneyThe paper account is cut into ten equal slices, one for each lane. A lane never spends another lane's slice.
Position sizeOne position is one twelfth of the lane's slice, in whole shares.
When it decidesAfter every trading day's close. The orders go to the broker a few minutes before the next day's close.
How many a nightAt most 3 new stocks. At most 12 stocks at once, and at most 6 in one sector. It never buys a stock it already holds.
When it sellsAt the close after its holding time: 5 or 20 trading days, given for each lane below. It sells earlier if the stock closes at or below 80% of its buying price.
Which stocksMost lanes look only at little-known quiet stocks. Quiet means the stock gets little attention that day. Little-known means its fame score, from its Wikipedia views over the past year, is at most 0.67 on a scale of 0 to 1.
LensA lens is a frozen forecast model built from the map's record. Five lanes read a lens, and the committee counts their votes. No lens has passed its test yet.
Judged againstRandom twins: 20 pretend accounts with the same limits that buy on the same nights, but pick their stocks at random from the same pool. A lane shows skill only if it beats its own twins. The S&P 500 is the second yardstick.
GatesA lane normally trades only after it passes two tests, called gates: a check on years held back for that purpose, and 60 days of live results. On 15 November 2026 the owner let these lanes trade on paper before passing either gate.
First orders17 November 2026.

The lanes

  1. 1Lens committee

    It buys a stock when at least two of six other lanes favour it on the same night.

    v(s) = ∑m = 16 1[s ∈ Lm]

    Lm is member m's list of its 10 best stocks that night. It buys every stock with v(s) ≥ 2 that it does not hold, if at least 3 of the 6 members have their data. Order: most votes, then the lowest sum of places on the lists.

    Looks for
    Votes. Six lanes each name their 10 best stocks: Company figures, Spreading attention, Aftershocks, Calm options, Flocking and Sector attention ahead. A stock on a list gets one vote.
    Buys
    Stocks with two votes or more, most votes first. If fewer than 3 voters have their data that night, it buys nothing.
    Holds
    20 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins and the S&P 500.

  2. 2Company figures

    It buys little-known quiet stocks of sound, cheap companies whose sales grow fastest.

    g = Slast 4 quarters / S4 quarters before − 1 ≥ 0

    Sound: g ≥ 0 and no miss in the newest earnings report (both rounded to 0.1 point). Cheap: in the top third of its sector by earnings yield E/P. Pool: fame ≤ 0.67 and quiet. It buys its 10 best by g, highest first.

    Looks for
    Sound companies: sales of the last four quarters at or above the four before, and no miss in the newest earnings report. Cheap companies: the cheapest third of their sector by earnings yield. It reads no lens.
    Buys
    Its 10 best by sales growth, fastest first. It is also one of the committee's six voters.
    Holds
    20 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins and the S&P 500.

  3. 3Spreading attention

    It buys quiet stocks that their links make likelier to turn loud than their own habit says.

    p = σ(b0 + b·xown + cnews log(1 + nnews) + cco log(1 + nco))

    p is the chance the stock turns loud within 5 trading days, σ the logistic curve. xown is the stock's own history: loud days and onsets in the last 60 trading days, tired today, fame, and the market's loud share. n counts the stocks among its 10 strongest links, by shared news or by moving together, that are loud today. The plain guess drops the two link terms. Score: plens − pplain > 0.

    Looks for
    The disease lens forecasts each day the chance that a stock turns loud. The lane takes that chance less the chance from the stock's own habit: the extra that its links add.
    Buys
    Its 10 best by that extra chance, only when the extra is above 0. It is also one of the committee's six voters.
    Holds
    5 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins and the S&P 500.
    So far

  4. 4Aftershocks

    It buys quiet stocks that the aftershocks lens expects to meet a new catalyst soon.

    K = log(1 + ∑d = 09 ∑j → i cj(t − d) e−d/τ), τ = 1

    cj(t − d) is 1 when linked stock j (one of the stock's 5 strongest links) had a catalyst d days ago. The lens puts K, one per kind of link, beside the stock's own catalyst rate, its own recent catalysts, its swing and the market's rate in a logistic model of the chance of a catalyst within 5 trading days. Score: plens − pplain > 0. The decay is exponential, not a power law.

    Looks for
    A catalyst is a burst of news or trading. The aftershocks lens forecasts each day the chance that a stock meets one. The lane takes that chance less the chance from the stock's own habit.
    Buys
    Its 10 best by that extra chance, only when the extra is above 0. It is also one of the committee's six voters.
    Holds
    5 trading days.

  5. 5Calm options

    It buys little-known quiet stocks whose options are priced calmest against the stock's own recent swings.

    score = −σimplied / σhistorical

    σimplied is how far option prices expect the stock to move; σhistorical is how far it moved lately. Only stocks wholly inside the lowest third of the pool's ratios that day can be picked. It buys its 10 calmest. Pool: fame ≤ 0.67 and quiet.

    Looks for
    Implied volatility (how much the option prices expect the stock to move) over historical volatility (how much it moved lately). Only the calmest third of the pool can be picked. It reads no lens.
    Buys
    Its 10 calmest, calmest first. It is also one of the committee's six voters.
    Holds
    20 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins and the S&P 500.

  6. 6Flocking

    It buys quiet stocks that the flocking lens expects to move up with their neighbours.

    fi = a rialign + c ricohere + s riseparate

    fi forecasts the stock's move over the next 20 trading days, in usual daily swings. Each r is a flocking rule measured on the stock's neighbours, scaled over the day's quiet little-known stocks, less what the stock's own heading and position explain. Frozen weights: a = 0.0039, c = −0.0184, s = 0.0001. It buys its 10 best with fi > 0.

    Looks for
    The flocking lens forecasts each week how far each stock will move, from how the stocks near it move. On other nights the lane reads the last week-end's forecast, at most four trading days old.
    Buys
    Its 10 best by forecast move, only when the move is above 0. It is also one of the committee's six voters.
    Holds
    20 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins and the S&P 500.

  7. 7Sector attention ahead

    It buys early into the sectors that the predator-prey lens expects to gain attention, through their little-known quiet stocks.

    Δ log x = α − βy − ρx log x  Δ log y = δx − γ − ρy log y

    A predator-prey model per sector, one step a week: y is the sector's share of loud stock-days (the predator), x the size of its moves (the prey). Run 4 weeks ahead, it gives ŷlens; the plain guess sets β = δ = 0 (the sector's own pull). Score: ŷlens − ŷown. It picks the 2 best sectors with a score above 0.

    Looks for
    The predator-prey lens forecasts each week every sector's share of loud stocks four weeks ahead. The lane picks the 2 sectors that beat their own usual share by the most.
    Buys
    The least-known quiet stocks of those 2 sectors, one from each in turn. If no sector beats its usual share, it buys nothing. It is also one of the committee's six voters.
    Holds
    20 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins and the S&P 500.

  8. 8Sector attention ahead, sound business only

    It takes the same list as Sector attention ahead, but skips companies whose business is getting worse.

    take s ⇔ g(s) ≥ 0.0 and surprise(s) ≥ 0

    g is sales growth over the last four quarters against the four before, and surprise is the newest earnings report against the estimate, both in percent rounded to 0.1 point. No report on record is not a miss; growth that cannot be worked out fails. It keeps its parent's order.

    Looks for
    Sales of the last four quarters at or above the four before, and no miss in the newest earnings report. A stock that fails is passed over and the next one is taken.
    Buys
    The sound stocks of its parent's list, in its parent's order.
    Holds
    20 trading days.
    Judged against
    Its parent lane only, over the days both ran. It has no random twins.

  9. 9Insider conviction

    It buys a stock after one of its officers or directors, who does not trade on a yearly routine, buys its shares on the open market.

    buy s ⇔ ∃ insider: ∑ $ ≥ 25,000 and not routine

    A buy counts when it is an open-market purchase on Form 4 by an officer or director, filed within 7 days and seen in the last 5 trading days. The insider must have traded the company's shares in each of the 3 calendar years before. Routine: a trade in the same calendar month in each of those 3 years. Order: most qualifying insiders, then the larger sum.

    Looks for
    Open-market buys on the SEC's Form 4, reported within 7 days and seen in the last 5 trading days. The insider must have traded the company's shares in each of the three years before. An insider who trades in the same month every year is on a routine and does not count.
    Buys
    Stocks with at least one such insider whose buys add up to $25,000 or more: most insiders first, then the larger sum. It looks at every stock, not only quiet ones, and reads no lens.
    Holds
    20 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins drawn from all stocks, and the S&P 500.
    So far

  10. 10News reader New

    An isolated AI model reads the news for stocks that a volatility model flags, and the lane buys the ones it expects to go up the next day.

    buy s ⇔ direction(s) = up and confidence(s) ≥ 52

    One isolated call to Claude Opus per flagged stock, with no tools and no memory. A failed or malformed answer gives no buy. Order: most confident first.

    Looks for
    Each evening a volatility model flags the 20 stocks it expects to be most active. Claude Opus reads each one's recent prices, headlines and linked stocks, with no tools and no memory, and gives a direction and a confidence from 0 to 100.
    Buys
    Flagged stocks called up for the next day with a confidence of 52 or more, most confident first.
    Holds
    5 trading days.
    Judged against
    20 random twins drawn from all stocks, the S&P 500, and the same reader with no news or another stock's news.
    Status
    Built and frozen. It has its own slice and has not bought yet, so it is not on the scoreboard. It does not rest on the map's lenses.

How a lane is judged

Each lane also keeps a pretend account that follows its rules exactly, with fills at the close. Its 20 random twins run beside it. A lane earns credit only if it beats its own twins, so luck and a rising market do not count as skill.

A lane reads "clearly ahead" only after at least 60 trading days and 20 closed trades, and only when it stands at least 2.5 standard deviations above its twins. Passing the live-results gate means only that a lane is not behind. The owner plans to judge the lanes at about 120 trading days.

Until then a lane says "too early to say". A few months of results are too few to tell skill from luck.
